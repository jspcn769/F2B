# F2B — BA Archetype Refinement
## Batch 1: Direction, creation, tempo and connection

**Document status:** REVISED_DRAFT  
**Scope:** BA-01, BA-02, BA-03, BA-15  
**Source:** F2B Master Archetype Catalogue, BA v1.0  
**Canonical status:** Not approved. This document refines the original draft without changing IDs or treating the profiles as final.

---

## 1. Governing rules

1. An archetype describes a stable basketball identity and dominant way of contributing; it is not an OVR tier, a quality judgment, or a rigid player class.
2. A player may display traits from several archetypes. The primary archetype identifies the most defining functional identity; secondary archetypes capture meaningful additional dimensions.
3. A role or tendency alone is insufficient. Classification must be supported by recurring responsibilities and observable actions, not by one isolated skill or a single match.
4. The A/B/C attribute groups are diagnostic indicators, not hard thresholds or OVR weights. The existing 36-attribute dictionary remains unchanged in this batch.
5. A player can be an excellent passer without being a Pure Playmaker; can direct an offense without being a Floor General; and can play slowly without being a Tempo Controller.

## 2. Family A — Direction and organization

The original family membership is retained: BA-01, BA-02, BA-03 and BA-15. The four identities are now separated by the principal question each one answers:

| ID | Defining question | Primary contribution |
|---|---|---|
| BA-01 Floor General | How should the whole offense function? | Overall orchestration and system direction |
| BA-02 Pure Playmaker | How can I create the best scoring chance for a teammate? | Primary creation through passing and vision |
| BA-03 Tempo Controller | At what speed and under what rhythm should we play? | Pace and possession-state management |
| BA-15 Connector Guard | How do I keep the action flowing after another player creates the advantage? | Secondary connection and continuity |

These are dominant identities, not mutually exclusive abilities.

# BA-01 — FLOOR GENERAL
**Catalan descriptor:** Director general del joc  
**Status:** REVISED_DRAFT  
**Family:** Direcció i organització

### Core identity
The Floor General is the primary on-court organizer of the team's offensive structure. He coordinates how possessions begin and develop, identifies the appropriate team option, keeps teammates connected to the system and adjusts the offense to the game situation.

His defining contribution is not a particular pass, scoring method or pace. It is the coherence of the offense as a whole. He may score, create, control tempo or play through screens, but none of those specialties is required to define him.

### Dominant functions
- Initiate and organize offensive possessions.
- Read the defensive alignment and select the appropriate team action.
- Coordinate teammates' spacing, timing and responsibilities.
- Keep the offense connected when the first option is denied.
- Recognize when to use transition, half-court structure or a mismatch.
- Manage late-game offensive organization and communication.

### Roles and subroles
- Primary offensive director.
- System organizer.
- Possession coordinator.
- On-court communicator and decision leader.

### Diagnostic attributes — retained from source
| Priority | Attributes |
|---|---|
| A — Nuclear | CRE-03, CRE-04, CRE-05, MND-01, MND-04 |
| B — Important | CRE-01, CRE-06, PHY-03, PHY-06, MND-05 |
| C — Complementary | OFF-05, OFF-06, DEF-01, MND-06 |

### Style and tendencies
- Frequently initiates or directs the first phase of possessions.
- Selects between different offensive options rather than forcing one preferred action.
- Distributes the ball according to team advantage.
- Can alternate between transition and half-court offense.
- Communicates and repositions teammates.
- Avoids unnecessary individual actions when a better team option exists.

### Strengths
- Overall offensive organization.
- Contextual and tactical reading.
- Coordination of teammates.
- Adaptability to different lineups and offensive systems.
- Reduction of structural errors and disjointed possessions.

### Limitations
- May not be an elite individual scorer or spectacular passer.
- Impact depends partly on teammates executing the selected actions.
- May create fewer advantages directly if lacking burst or advanced isolation skills.
- Can become overly system-dependent if unable to improvise.

### Tactical fit
Best suited to teams that need a clear primary organizer, coherent half-court structure, coordinated decision-making and a player who can keep multiple offensive options connected.

### Boundary rules
- **Versus BA-02 Pure Playmaker:** BA-01 directs the whole offense; BA-02 is defined by exceptional creation for teammates through passing and vision. A Floor General may be a good passer without being an elite chance creator.
- **Versus BA-03 Tempo Controller:** BA-01 decides how the offense should function; BA-03 specializes in the pace and rhythm at which it functions.
- **Versus BA-15 Connector Guard:** BA-01 is normally a primary organizer; BA-15 is most valuable as a secondary link within an offense whose primary advantage or direction often comes from another player.

### Classification test
Assign BA-01 when the player's most defining contribution is organizing and directing the team's overall offense across different situations—not merely recording assists, handling the ball frequently or preferring a slow pace.

# BA-02 — PURE PLAYMAKER
**Catalan descriptor:** Creador i passador de màxim nivell  
**Status:** REVISED_DRAFT  
**Family:** Direcció i organització

### Core identity
The Pure Playmaker is a primary offensive creator whose standout skill is producing high-quality scoring opportunities for teammates. He manipulates defenders with the ball, anticipates movement, identifies passing windows and delivers the ball at the moment and angle that turn a potential opening into a real advantage.

His defining output is teammate opportunity creation. He can control a large share of possessions and may be the team's main creator, but he is not automatically the team's best overall organizer or pace manager.

### Dominant functions
- Create high-quality shots for teammates.
- Read and manipulate defensive rotations.
- Deliver precise passes into interior, perimeter and transition opportunities.
- Find teammates before or as they become open.
- Create advantages through passing angles, timing and deception.
- Coordinate actions involving finishers, cutters and rolling bigs.

### Roles and subroles
- Primary playmaker.
- Pass-first creator.
- Advanced vision passer.
- Advantage creator through distribution.
- Interior and perimeter facilitator.

### Diagnostic attributes — retained from source
| Priority | Attributes |
|---|---|
| A — Nuclear | CRE-03, CRE-04, CRE-05, CRE-01 |
| B — Important | CRE-06, MND-01, MND-04, MND-05 |
| C — Complementary | PHY-03, PHY-06, OFF-06, OFF-07 |

### Style and tendencies
- Looks to create for teammates before seeking his own shot when the defense offers that option.
- Uses ball-handling and changes of rhythm to open passing lanes.
- Finds cutters, rollers and weak-side shooters.
- Can deliver difficult or creative passes when the advantage justifies the risk.
- Maintains a high level of involvement in offensive creation.
- May use scoring threat to make passing options more effective.

### Strengths
- Vision and passing variety.
- Creation of high-value opportunities.
- Manipulation of defensive positioning.
- Ability to improve the offensive output of finishers and cutters.
- Ability to create without needing a high scoring volume.

### Limitations
- May offer less individual scoring pressure than a scoring-first guard.
- Can become turnover-prone if attempting overly ambitious passes.
- May depend on teammates converting the opportunities created.
- Can be targeted by aggressive pressure if ball security or physical resistance is insufficient.

### Tactical fit
Best suited to teams with reliable finishers, mobile interior targets, cutters and shooters who can convert the opportunities generated by a high-volume primary creator.

### Boundary rules
- **Versus BA-01 Floor General:** BA-02's defining excellence is creating shots for others; BA-01's is organizing the entire offense. A Pure Playmaker can be a less complete system manager, while a Floor General need not be an extraordinary passer.
- **Versus BA-15 Connector Guard:** BA-02 creates the initial advantage and often requires the ball; BA-15 preserves and extends an advantage created by someone else.
- **Versus BA-04 Pick-and-Roll Maestro:** BA-02's creation is not dependent on one tactical action. BA-04 is specifically defined by reading and exploiting pick-and-roll coverages.

### Classification test
Assign BA-02 when elite teammate creation through passing and vision is the player's central offensive identity—not simply because he has good assist numbers or is trusted to bring the ball up.

# BA-03 — TEMPO CONTROLLER
**Catalan descriptor:** Controlador del ritme del partit  
**Status:** REVISED_DRAFT  
**Family:** Direcció i organització

### Core identity
The Tempo Controller manages the speed, rhythm and volatility of the game. He recognizes when the team should accelerate, when it should organize, when to extend a possession, when to attack early and when to reduce risk or protect a lead.

This archetype is not synonymous with slow play. A Tempo Controller may deliberately accelerate a game when the opponent is disorganized or slow it when the team needs control. The defining skill is choosing and imposing the appropriate rhythm—not preferring one fixed pace.

### Dominant functions
- Regulate offensive pace according to game state.
- Recognize when transition is advantageous and when it should be stopped.
- Prevent rushed or low-value possessions.
- Manage possession length and late-clock situations.
- Reduce unnecessary volatility when protecting a lead.
- Change rhythm to disrupt the opponent's preferred game.
- Stabilize the team under pressure.

### Roles and subroles
- Pace manager.
- Possession stabilizer.
- Game-state controller.
- Late-game tempo manager.
- Transition trigger and brake.

### Diagnostic attributes — retained from source
| Priority | Attributes |
|---|---|
| A — Nuclear | MND-01, MND-04, MND-05, CRE-05 |
| B — Important | CRE-03, CRE-04, PHY-06, CRE-01 |
| C — Complementary | PHY-01, OFF-05, OFF-06, MND-06 |

### Style and tendencies
- Varies pace deliberately rather than following a fixed preference.
- Recognizes opportunities to run before the defense is set.
- Can slow down and organize when transition is not favorable.
- Uses possession time according to score, opponent and game phase.
- Limits avoidable turnovers and rushed decisions.
- Values control in high-pressure and late-game situations.

### Strengths
- Game management.
- Rhythm variation.
- Possession stability.
- Contextual decision-making.
- Ability to protect or recover a desired game state.

### Limitations
- May over-control and miss opportunities to attack early.
- Can reduce offensive dynamism if too conservative.
- Needs teammates capable of executing different rhythms.
- May have less value in a system that demands constant maximum pace regardless of context.

### Tactical fit
Useful in teams that need to manage game state, alternate between transition and half-court play, protect leads, reduce opponent runs or control high-pressure possessions.

### Boundary rules
- **Versus BA-01 Floor General:** BA-03's specialist contribution is pace and rhythm management; BA-01's is overall offensive organization. A player can be a strong organizer without being an exceptional tempo manager.
- **Versus BA-09 Transition Engine:** BA-09 generates value by consistently exploiting open-court situations; BA-03 decides whether accelerating is the correct choice and can deliberately suppress transition.
- **Versus BA-15 Connector Guard:** BA-03 controls the pace of the possession; BA-15 connects actions within the possession.

### Classification test
Assign BA-03 only when the player's repeated, observable ability to regulate pace and game state is a defining strength. A slow-playing guard, a cautious passer or a high-IQ player is not automatically a Tempo Controller.

# BA-15 — CONNECTOR GUARD
**Catalan descriptor:** Base connector i facilitador  
**Status:** REVISED_DRAFT  
**Family:** Direcció i organització

### Core identity
The Connector Guard is a low-to-moderate-usage guard who keeps the offense connected between its primary creators and finishers. He makes quick, low-error decisions, moves the ball to the next advantage, relocates without the ball and can assume secondary handling responsibilities without needing to dominate possessions.

His value lies in continuity and complementary decision-making. He is not simply a less talented playmaker: he performs a distinct function in lineups where another player initiates or creates the main advantage.

### Dominant functions
- Move the ball quickly to the next useful action.
- Connect primary creators with scorers and interior finishers.
- Make secondary reads after the defense rotates.
- Relocate and create spacing after passing.
- Provide secondary ball-handling and initiation.
- Keep possessions alive without unnecessary dribbling.
- Support multi-creator lineups without competing for primary control.

### Roles and subroles
- Offensive connector.
- Secondary playmaker.
- Second-side creator.
- Off-ball guard and relocation player.
- Auxiliary organizer.

### Diagnostic attributes — retained from source
| Priority | Attributes |
|---|---|
| A — Nuclear | CRE-03, CRE-05, MND-01, MND-04, MND-05 |
| B — Important | CRE-04, CRE-06, CRE-01, OFF-06, PHY-03 |
| C — Complementary | PHY-06, DEF-01, MND-02, MND-06 |

### Style and tendencies
- Moves the ball quickly rather than holding it unnecessarily.
- Makes the next pass when another player has created the advantage.
- Moves and relocates after passing.
- Contributes through off-ball positioning and spacing.
- Can run a secondary action or lead a second unit.
- Prioritizes continuity and sound decisions over individual possession volume.

### Strengths
- Decision speed and reliability.
- Offensive continuity.
- Fit alongside high-usage creators.
- Secondary creation and ball security.
- Tactical flexibility and off-ball contribution.

### Limitations
- May not create enough advantage to lead an offense alone.
- Can have low scoring or assist totals despite positive team impact.
- Depends on primary creators to generate the initial advantage.
- May be underused in systems that require one guard to dominate the ball.

### Tactical fit
Best suited to teams with several creators, star scorers, rapid ball movement, second-side actions, double-guard lineups and offenses that distribute initiation responsibilities.

### Boundary rules
- **Versus BA-01 Floor General:** BA-01 is the primary director; BA-15 is generally a secondary connector.
- **Versus BA-02 Pure Playmaker:** BA-02 creates the initial advantage through high-level passing and vision; BA-15 connects the next action after an advantage has been created.
- **Versus BA-07 Combo Guard:** BA-07 is defined by a genuine hybrid identity between point guard and shooting guard responsibilities; BA-15 is defined by connective function and low need for possession, not by scoring/position hybridity.
- **Versus BA-03 Tempo Controller:** BA-03 manages pace; BA-15 maintains action continuity.

### Classification test
Assign BA-15 when the player's most valuable recurring contribution is connecting other creators and finishers through quick decisions, secondary handling and off-ball movement—not when he is merely a low-usage player or a good passer.

## 3. Cross-archetype decision rules

Use the following questions in order when distinguishing these four profiles:

1. Does the player primarily organize the entire offense? Consider BA-01.
2. Is his standout contribution creating high-quality shots for teammates through exceptional passing and vision? Consider BA-02.
3. Is his defining advantage regulating pace and game state across different rhythms? Consider BA-03.
4. Does he mainly connect and extend advantages generated by another primary creator? Consider BA-15.

If two answers are both strongly supported, retain one primary and one secondary archetype rather than forcing a false distinction. The primary selection must reflect the player's most defining and recurring responsibility in the relevant season.
